Shipped inside the product, Shopify Payments ran 67% of GMV in Q1 2026 (SEC 10-Q, first-party audited — 64% a year earlier). Both sit inside the "20% to 90+%" vertical-SaaS spread published by Ronnie Gurion, COO of Clio, who also names the driver: payments as a core product in quotas → "maximum attach"; a SPIFF → it "still gets treated like a secondary product." That is exactly the difference between MLE shipping a site that transacts and MLE referring a merchant. Stripe's 200+-company 2025 benchmark corroborates direction (median attach "has doubled in 1 year"), weighted medium — the absolute median is unpublished and was not guessed. Toast's ~100% is a contrast, not a benchmark (attach by contractual requirement).
The bound, printed not buried: there is no published figure for our actual mode — third-party-built site, chatbot takes the payment, merchant boarded on the builder's Schedule A. Shopify is a first-party platform monetizing its own checkout, so 67% is a directional ceiling, never a forecast, and the sub-20% denominator below stands as the planning number. Blending 67% with 20% into a "realistic" attach would be a fabricated statistic on a money model — refused deliberately, and the refusal is written into the DD so no later pass can quietly invent it.
Scope — corrected by Rob, same evening Not a vertical pick — a horizontal capability. The embedded chatbot rides the websites MLE spins up, across every vertical; AIVA gets trained per vertical (e-commerce is one training target, and apparel was just the example). The capability is constant, the money moment varies: product purchase (retail) · deposit on a booked job (contractors) · fee payment (title/RE) · invoice or retainer (services). The design principle is Amazon's Buy Now button next to Add to Cart — fewer steps, payment collected before the customer ever reaches a checkout page. Rob's three reasons: convenience to the customer, more conversions for the business owner, a profit center for MLE.
💡 "ISO or PayFac or whatever?" — the direct answer You do not need to be either to take a piece of the processing profit. The residual comes from the Schedule A, not the rung — rung 1 pays it at $0 on every merchant boarded on your code. What PFaaS buys, and neither the agent nor the registered-ISO rung does, is owning the in-chat checkout and programming the charge (and with it the switching cost). Sequential, not either/or. Gap named: PFaaS revenue-share economics are unsourced — that comparison is not honest until they are, and it is now a DD checklist line.
🔑 Resale value re-ranks the non-negotiables Rob cites portfolio resale (25–40× monthly) as a reason for the whole initiative, so NN#3 — third-party portfolio sale rights — is now the deal-breaker clause, not the third checkbox. §3d already caught the trap: SignaPay's published "portfolio buyouts" means SignaPay as buyer. A book you may only sell back to the processor does not carry the outside multiple.
🔒 Engineering constraint for the spec Collecting payment inside an LLM-driven chat means card data must never enter the model's context or our logs — hosted field / hosted page / tokenized element only (Square payment links, Helcim HelcimPay.js), bot sees a token and a status.
