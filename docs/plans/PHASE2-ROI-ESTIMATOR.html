Revenue lift starts at $0 on every row, on purpose. The labor half is sourced (BLS wages × hours you can defend); the revenue half is judgement, so nothing is claimed until a human types it or clicks the button above. Hours/week and revenue lift are editable — change any cell and the whole page re-runs. One property to know: with fixed rates every term scales linearly with the day count, so moving the days slider changes the dollars, not the percentage — the % only moves when you change hours, lift, or the investment. Hourly rates are not assumptions: they are BLS published medians for the selected region, linked per row. Where BLS publishes no metro figure the row says which level it fell back to, rather than passing a national number off as local.

The formula, as Rob wrote it

Phase 2 Investment = ROI Target value to date = (labor hours saved × labor cost/hr) + revenue since Phase 2 start target to date = Investment ÷ 91 × days elapsed ROI % = value to date ÷ target to date − 1 ROI $ = value to date − target to date

Surplus reads green, shortfall reads red, and the target never exceeds the full investment — past day 91 it stops growing rather than pretending the guarantee keeps compounding.

Open — flagged, not silently decided

  • "Total Revenue since start of Phase 2" — total, or attributable? Taken here as attributable revenue. Crediting Phase 2 with the client's entire top line would clear the guarantee on day one for any business already making money, which would make the number meaningless to show them. Say the word and it flips.
  • Where do the actuals come from? Estimates are what you sell on; the running ROI needs real hours-saved and revenue per client. That is the plumbing Q40's component webhooks were already going to carry — worth wiring the same seam rather than a second one.
  • Phase 2 price is open (Rob, 7/25 — partner is writing the pricing logic). The $9,100 default here is a placeholder chosen so the per-day target reads cleanly at $100/day.
  • Revenue-lift figures are the softest inputs on the page — they are per-automation judgement, not published data, which is exactly why every one of them is an editable field and why the labor half is sourced separately.
