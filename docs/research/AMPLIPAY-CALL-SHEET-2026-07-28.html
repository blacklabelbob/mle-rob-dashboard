The gate — no Schedule A review is worth doing before these three land in writing

Say this up front: “Before we look at a Schedule A, I need three things in writing — the sponsor bank, the entity that counter-signs, and what you publish for disclosure. Everything else follows from those.” All four non-negotiables are claims against whoever’s paper the residual sits on; until that party is named, the terms describe nothing.

Gate 1 · the only capitalized party in the chain

Name the sponsor / acquiring bank and the acquiring BIN, in writing — and send me the ISO/MSP disclosure language you publish.

No sponsor, acquiring or member bank is named on any of five live properties. Visa/Mastercard require the “[Company] is a registered ISO/MSP of [Bank], [City], [State]” disclosure on website and marketing material — and across five properties of a self-described bankcard ISO it appears nowhere. Absence from marketing is not proof there is no sponsor bank; what it establishes is that the bank is unlearnable from the public record and the required disclosure is missing.

Q6 · which channel my book gets boarded through

Would a roofing / title SMB portfolio board as an ordinary ISO or as HR-ISO (high-risk)?

High-risk acquiring pays the fattest agent splits — plausibly what makes this attractive — and carries the worst attrition, chargeback exposure and reserve/clawback posture. A normal SMB book boarded through a high-risk channel inherits that channel’s reserve and pricing posture. It also changes non-negotiable #2: “no production minimums that claw” is a different question against a high-risk portfolio.
