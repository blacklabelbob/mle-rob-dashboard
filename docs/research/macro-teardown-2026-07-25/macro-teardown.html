Macro, read end to end

Eight parallel agents read the whole thing — 1.09 million lines, 164 Rust crates, 42 services — against the MLE CRM. This is what's worth taking, what's a mirage, and the one legal fact that decides the merge question.

The decision

Do not merge Macro. Do not port its code. Read it, then build your own. The blocker isn't taste or effort — it's AGPL §13. The moment a roofing contractor or title company logs into a CRM containing Macro-derived code, you owe that customer the complete source of your modified version, including your own proprietary logic.

That's not a technicality you can engineer around. Translating Rust to TypeScript is still a derivative work — translation is expressly covered by copyright. And your standing “no upstream attribution” rule is legally impossible here: AGPL's attribution and source-offer duties are the licence's whole enforcement mechanism, not a courtesy you can strip like you would with MIT.

The good news is that almost nothing you actually want from Macro is Macro's code. It's their decisions — a permission table shape, a dedup pipeline, an email-to-company rule, a motion curve. Ideas aren't copyrightable. Take all of them.

The tell

Macro's own funded team is running production Postgres 14 against dev 16, with publicly reachable RDS instances.

One founder plus agents can build something like this. Nobody solo can operate it. That, not the code quality, is the real argument against adopting their architecture wholesale.

The licence wall

Macro relicensed from BSL to AGPL-3.0 on 31 May 2026 — weeks ago. There's no CLA, so contributions stay AGPL and even Macro can't relicense them. They run commercial licensing (licensing@macro.com) as a named revenue line, so expect a real sales conversation, not silence.

Why “internal only” is a trap, not a loophole

AGPL's own text says “mere interaction with a user through a computer network, with no transfer of a copy, is not conveying,” and your own W-2 reps aren't “other parties.” So internal-only use genuinely doesn't trigger §13 — today. The trap is that your business model is selling this to clients. The day a contractor logs in, or you white-label an instance onto a title company's site, the obligation fires retroactively on everything you built on top. You'd be choosing, years in, between open-sourcing your product and ripping out its foundations.

The free lunch — take this part with both hands

AGPL attaches to Macro's own code, not to what Macro depends on. These are real, current dependencies in their manifests, and all of them are yours to use with zero exposure:

LiveKit (Apache-2.0) · Lexical (MIT, Meta) · Loro CRDT (MIT) · Deepgram (a paid API, not code at all). Their loro-mirror package isn't even Macro IP — it's an MIT fork of upstream that they plan to delete.

Worth a real lawyer's hour

  • Does an internal-only deployment that later gains one external user create retroactive obligations on the intervening builds?
  • Where exactly is the line between a clean-room reimplementation and a derivative work when the same person read the source?
  • What would a commercial licence from Macro actually cost, and does it survive an acquisition of your company?

Standard caveat: none of this is legal advice, and the agents that produced it aren't lawyers.

Myths vs. the source

The most valuable thing this teardown produced isn't a list of things to copy. It's the list of things you were impressed by that turn out not to exist. Every one of these was checked in code, not documentation.

“Shared team-level memory, everything @linked and queryable”
It's a TEXT column. pub type Memory = String, one row per user, LLM-written, regenerated when stale. Not team-shared. Not a graph. Not a vector store.
“AI that never loses context” — i.e. serious RAG
There is no RAG. OpenSearch holds zero vectors; search is keyword matching sorted by updated_at, with the relevance score computed and then discarded. The entire embedding stack exists for one feature: duplicate task detection.
“The fastest interface” — local-first, CRDT-powered
CRDTs touch only the markdown editor. The ambitious local-first cache is behind a feature flag, unfinished, and its stated goal is bounded memory, not speed. Roughly 20% architecture, 80% frontend craft — and the 80% ports cleanly to React.
“Calls” as a telephony feature
Zero SIP, zero PSTN, zero DTMF in the entire repo. They have no phone system. “Calls” are in-app WebRTC meetings on LiveKit Cloud.
A CRM with pipelines and deals
There is no deal object — the company is the deal, so they can't model two jobs at one address. No activities table. No merge UI. Their CRM list caps at 500 rows and filters client-side.
An agent framework worth studying
They didn't write an agent loop — crates/agent is an adapter over a forked rig-core. Structured output is prompted JSON, never validated against its schema. Prompt caching fields exist and are never set (the code comment reads // who cares).
What this means

Macro's moat isn't AI or CRDTs. It's boring and real: one Postgres, one flattened permission table, and one toolset reused by chat, bots, projections and MCP alike.

You were right to be impressed — but by the plumbing, not the magic. That's good news, because plumbing is exactly what you can rebuild.

Where you're already ahead

Worth saying plainly, because the instinct after reading a $30m codebase is to assume you're behind everywhere. On CRM specifically, you are not.

  • A real deals table with 12 stages. Macro has none — one company, one stage.
  • Explainable weighted scoring that ships its breakdown. They have frecency built and never exposed in the UI.
  • A DB-enforced e-sign audit chain — nothing comparable exists there.
  • Referral lineage that refuses to guess, and typed intake source_context.
  • Full-text search. Macro deleted theirs.
  • Dedupe that refuses to merge money-carrying records.
  • An activities table anchored to person/org/deal, with summary, action_items and buying_signals columns already waiting. Macro anchors calls to channels and has no call-to-company link at all.
  • Your inline click-to-edit kit is genuinely Attio-grade and beats anything in their repo. Don't touch it.

Your problem was never features. It's that you have eight strong vertical features and no horizontal spine tying them together — which is precisely the one thing Macro does better than anyone.

Ranked by value ÷ effort. Every one of these is “take the design, retype the code” — none involves pasting a line of theirs.

Explicitly don't copy

  • The 42-service split and nine datastores — that's a 20-person org's shape, not yours.
  • The 164-crate explosion (57 crates are under 300 lines — that's Rust compile management, not architecture).
  • Company-as-deal. It's a regression from what you already have.
  • Their CRDT sync, WASM browser cache, and SharedWorker topology.
  • Self-hosted FusionAuth, and their REST+GraphQL dual transport.

The UI kit

You said the CRM looks raggedy. It mostly isn't — it's under-finished in four specific, cheap ways. Three of them shipped tonight.

Voice & calls

Your instinct was right, and there's now hard evidence for it: an a16z-backed team building exactly this picked LiveKit + Deepgram independently.

  • Transport is LiveKit Cloud — livekit-api in Rust, livekit-client in the browser, plus their Krisp noise filter. Apache-2.0, so free of any AGPL concern.
  • Transcription is Deepgram nova-3, streaming — but proxied through LiveKit Inference, which takes a margin. Go direct to Deepgram and pay less.
  • Steal endpointing: 400. Their code comment says the 25ms library default “cuts hesitant speakers mid-thought.” Prospects hesitate constantly.
  • Skip their recording architecture. RoomComposite egress runs a headless-Chrome compositor per room — necessary for multi-party video, absurd for a two-party phone call. Twilio's dual-channel recording gives you better speaker separation than their inference-based diarization, for free.
  • They have zero real-time in-call AI (audio_output=False). Your live sales coach isn't something to copy from them — but the hard part, the streaming pipe, is proven.
  • No retention policy on recordings at all — no S3 lifecycle rule anywhere. Don't inherit that; Florida is a two-party-consent state.
Your dialer is further along than you think

Softphone, token minting, TwiML dual-channel recording and a signature-checked webhook are all built and env-gated. The gap is one line: the recording webhook currently only console.logs its payload.

Next three moves — persist that payload as a dialer activity via the existing phone matcher; add a segment-granular transcripts table (not a text blob — segments are what enable moment search and playback sync); then Deepgram prerecorded plus one model call into the summary / buying_signals columns you already have.

Full costed comparison — Twilio vs LiveKit SIP vs Telnyx vs the SaaS dialers, recording law by state, and the live-coach architecture — is landing in a companion decision doc.
