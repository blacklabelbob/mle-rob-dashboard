    [paragraph] Discovery call between Rob (STG) and Chai, owner of BBX Moving Company (commercial and residential moving, ~14 years in market). Chai attended STG’s recruiting webinar and wanted to explore whether STG could help him break through a flat revenue ceiling and add a true commercial business development producer.
    [heading_3] Company Snapshot (BBX Moving Company)
    [bulleted_list_item] Service mix: ~60% commercial moving (offices, warehouses, government agencies), ~40% residential.
    [bulleted_list_item] Revenue: ~$2.0–$2.2M per year for the last 3 years (2023–2025), essentially flat.
    [bulleted_list_item] 2026 goal: ~20% growth to ~$2.6M, with most upside expected from the commercial side.
    [bulleted_list_item] Sales structure: 3-person sales team — 1 commercial-focused rep, 1 residential-focused rep, and Chai covering both but limited time in sales.
    [bulleted_list_item] Close rates: ~75% commercial, ~50% residential.
    [heading_3] Current Go-To-Market & Lead Flow
    [paragraph] Residential
    [bulleted_list_item] Mostly inbound: people find BBX via online presence and marketing.
    [bulleted_list_item] Channels: online search, letters, referrals from realtors, brand recognition built over 14 years.
    [bulleted_list_item] Dynamics: higher volume, lower average job size (~$2K–$10K), more competition and price sensitivity.
    [paragraph] Commercial
    [bulleted_list_item] Mixed inbound and outbound, but growth is constrained primarily by top-of-funnel lead volume, not by closing ability.
    [bulleted_list_item] Average job size: ~$11K on the low end up to ~$200K on the high end.
    [bulleted_list_item] Close rate: ~75% once BBX gets in to quote opportunities.
    [bulleted_list_item] Market structure: limited direct competitors (about 5–6 commercial movers in the city), and projects are time-sensitive and complex — which favors BBX’s expertise and contributes to high close rates.
    [bulleted_list_item] Key relationship channel: large commercial tenant improvement (TI) and renovation contractors who are offering “turnkey” build-out plus move-in solutions. BBX is often brought in as a subcontractor or referral partner to execute the move.
    [bulleted_list_item] These GC/TI relationships are valuable but lumpy: each contractor may only have one or two active projects at a time, so Chai needs many relationships to produce enough opportunities.
    [bulleted_list_item] Important nuance: once BBX is in the door via these partners, they are still selling and quoting to the end customer, not the GC, and they are good at converting those opportunities.
    [heading_3] Existing Outbound Campaigns (Commercial)
    [numbered_list_item] Purchased commercial moving lead lists + cold calling
      [bulleted_list_item] Buys ~80 leads/month from two 3rd-party providers that specialize in commercial moving leads (North America, UK, Australia, etc.).
      [bulleted_list_item] Data quality issues: 30–40% of leads are dead (already moved, not moving, plans changed).
      [bulleted_list_item] Competitive pressure: lists are sold to several other local movers (3–4 competitors also calling the same accounts).
      [bulleted_list_item] Workflow: leads go into CRM → BBX sends letters → a virtual cold caller works the list to set appointments for in-person estimates.
      [bulleted_list_item] Yield: from ~80 raw leads → ~50 viable → ~10 actual quoting opportunities → BBX closes ~60–70% of those.
      [bulleted_list_item] Challenge: timing and sales cycle length cause uneven flow; some months only 4–5 opportunities, other months ~10. Overall ROI is modest and inconsistent.
    [numbered_list_item] Relationship-based business development with commercial TI/renovation contractors
      [bulleted_list_item] Chai invests time building relationships with commercial TI and renovation general contractors that manage office and warehouse buildouts.
      [bulleted_list_item] These firms want to be turnkey providers, so they often bring BBX in as a subcontractor or referral partner to handle the move portion.
      [bulleted_list_item] This has produced solid opportunities over the last year, but is inherently slow and inconsistent because each GC only has a few active projects.
      [bulleted_list_item] No material involvement from commercial roofing partners; primary focus is TI/renovation contractors.
    [heading_3] Strategic Challenges (From Chai’s Perspective)
    [bulleted_list_item] Growth Ceiling: Stuck at ~$2.0–$2.2M revenue for 3+ years; wants to reach ~$2.6M with commercial as the primary growth engine.
    [bulleted_list_item] Top-of-Funnel Constraint (Commercial): Close rate is strong once they are in front of opportunities, but there are not enough at-bats.
    [bulleted_list_item] Lead Source Quality and Consistency:
      [bulleted_list_item] Purchased lists are expensive, shared with competitors, and contain a high percentage of dead leads.
      [bulleted_list_item] TI/GC referrals are high-quality but lumpy and hard to scale.
    [bulleted_list_item] Capacity vs. Demand: Operationally, BBX is not at capacity. They could handle more commercial work if the pipeline were healthier.
    [bulleted_list_item] People Problem – Commercial BD/Relationship Builder:
      [bulleted_list_item] Chai sees a big opportunity in adding a “rock star” commercial BD hire who can deepen and expand relationships and consistently create opportunities.
      [bulleted_list_item] Budget and risk are the bottlenecks: the market seems to demand a high-base, experienced BD profile (~$100K+ base), but ramp times are long and there is no guarantee of quick results.
      [bulleted_list_item] The ideal candidate is someone credible with GCs and construction-related stakeholders, which often means they are already embedded in larger, more stable companies.
    [bulleted_list_item] Role Design Ambiguity:
      [bulleted_list_item] Two possible paths:
        [numbered_list_item] Hire a new BD-focused producer to do what Chai currently does (networking, relationship-building, higher-level BD).
        [numbered_list_item] Move the existing commercial sales rep into more of a BD role and backfill with a sales estimator, increasing leverage on current relationships.
      [bulleted_list_item] The right path depends on the talent available in the market (i.e., “Michael Jordan in the draft” analogy) and Chai’s comfort with comp structure.
