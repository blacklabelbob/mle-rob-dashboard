# Roofing — The Definitive Go-to-Market Strategy (AI-Native Roofing Technology Arsenal)

**Vertical:** Roofing · **Status:** DRAFT (Rob 2026-07-08: "not finished by any means") · **Added to dashboard:** 2026-07-08
**Method spine:** Crawford EDP→PVP / Cannonball GTM (see `~/.claude/rules/crawford-gtm.md`) · **Companion docs:** `MLE-FUNNEL-MAP.md`, `REFERRAL-DISCOVERY-ENGINE.md`, Storm Match (storm-match.vercel.app)
**Live accounts this strategy already touches:** CG Roofing Group (SIGNED 6/22, $10k Phase One), roofing cluster (12 mapped DFW/FL companies)

## TL;DR
- **Lead with a hybrid: storm-intelligence-led direct product-led growth (Route A) as the primary engine, immediately paired with 1–2 lean mega-contractor tech-for-equity deals (Route C) as proof labs, then layer a supplier/manufacturer co-brand (Route B) and a single gutter-PE operating-partner deal (Route D) as force multipliers in years 2–4.** Pure white-label to a giant and pure PE-platform licensing are traps if done first — they capture your moat before you've built brand power.
- **The storm-intelligence system is the correct tip of the spear** — it produces the fastest trust and case studies in the fastest-moving buyer network in the industry — but you must gate it behind a "professional restoration" customer filter or it will saturate you with churn-and-burn storm chasers who destroy the long-term platform brand.
- **The founder's three most fragile assumptions are: (1) "my tech can't be replicated" — false; the individual capabilities exist in the market and incumbents are well-funded, so your moat is integration + data + speed, not any single feature; (2) "white-label under my terms" — achievable only while you hold leverage (working tech + outcomes they can't yet replicate), which is an 18–36 month window, not permanent; (3) "suppliers will become heroes" — partially true, but distributors move slowly and will co-brand, not cede value; treat them as distribution, not saviors.**

## Key Findings

**Market context (validated).** The U.S. roofing services industry is roughly $99.8 billion (IBISWorld, 2025), across 100,000+ contractors where the top 100 hold under 10% share and the largest single player (Tecta America) holds ~1.7%. PE has gone from 17 roofing platforms in early 2023 to 56 by end of 2024, with 134 contractor acquisitions in 2024 — roughly one platform deal every 48 hours by mid-2025. Roofing software is a fragmented ~$900M niche led by JobNimbus and AccuLynx, both of which are only now bolting on shallow AI. Sumeru Equity Partners announced a $330M control investment in JobNimbus on Nov 13, 2024 (a "game-changing SaaS provider for the roofing industry... $330 million growth investment"), with founder/CEO Ben Hodson and Mainsail Partners retaining equity; the company serves 8,000+ contractors.

**AI adoption gap is real and closing fast.** Per BNP Media/myCLEARopinion's "2026 State of the Roofing Industry" report (Roofing Contractor), "Artificial intelligence use has grown, with 40% of contractors currently using it in 2025 compared to 29% in 2024," with another 36% discussing implementation over the next two years. Yet per a 2025 National Roofing Contractors Association survey (cited by CallSetter AI), "only 21% of roofing contractors use any form of AI... The remaining 79% rely entirely on manual processes for answering phones, scheduling estimates, and following up with leads." The window for an AI-native entrant is 18–36 months before incumbents (JobNimbus, AccuLynx, ServiceTitan) close it with embedded features.

**Storm economics are enormous and accelerating.** Severe convective storms cost global insurers about $208 billion over 2023–2025, of which roughly $176B (85%) occurred in the U.S., per Gallagher Re / Allianz Commercial (April 2026), which calls SCS "a dominant annual loss driver for the industry"; hail alone typically accounts for 50%–80% of SCS claims. Per the State Farm newsroom, "In 2024, customers nationwide experienced property damage from severe hail, leading State Farm to pay out more than $3.8 billion for home repairs and over $1.2 billion for auto repairs" (Texas alone $1.1B; State Farm's 2025 hail total rose to $5.6B). ZestyAI's Z-HAIL model finds "more than 12.6 million U.S. properties are at high risk of hail-related roof damage, representing $189.5 billion in potential replacement costs"; Cotality's 2026 report counts 43.5 million U.S. properties facing moderate-or-greater hail risk. Texas, Kansas, Nebraska, Colorado, and the Hail Alley tri-state corner are the geographic core. Contractors pay $80–$500 per lead; exclusive storm appointments run ~$200 each (e.g., Call Center Doctors at $200/appointment, HailTrace-verified). The 1.5-inch hail threshold the founder's system targets is exactly the industry-recognized damage threshold (Gallagher Re).

**Storm-intelligence incumbents are beatable on speed but entrenched on trust.** HailTrace (15+ meteorologists, "call for pricing," ~$1,999/yr tier reported), Hail Recon/Interactive Hail Maps ("within minutes," data to 2011), and the insurance-grade forensic players (CoreLogic/Cotality, Verisk/Respond/Benchmark) all deliver swaths within 2–5 minutes of radar scans but are oriented to canvassing maps or insurer forensics — none package detection → geolocation → permit-based roof-age/material cross-reference → probability score → qualified lead in one automated 2-minute pipeline. That specific integration is genuinely differentiated.

**PE platforms buy operational tech and pay for it.** ServiceTitan built an explicit PE program ("500+ customers with $100M+ revenue"), and roofing platforms (Omnia/CCMP, Vertex/Alpine, Valor/Osceola, Infinity, Stronghouse, Best Choice/Brightstar) are actively professionalizing. Powering these consolidators directly contradicts the pro-independence positioning — the honest answer is that Route D is a revenue opportunity that dilutes the brand promise, so it must be sequenced late and firewalled.

**The gutter PE angle is a genuine, low-competition proof lab.** Named active consolidators include CID Capital (Etruscan Gutters & Roofing, Fund IV's first platform, Aug 2025), Riverside/Evive Brands (The Brothers That Just Do Gutters, 300+ territories, publicly implementing a new CRM), IBP (Gutter Pro, June 2024), Great Day Improvements/Wafra (LeafGuard + Englert from Audax, June 2024), and Leaf Home/Gridiron (LeafFilter, ~$1.8B+ revenue, merged with Erie Home Sept 2025). The gutter tech gap is specific and real: no gutter-native aerial linear-footage measurement standard, generic CRMs ill-fit to per-linear-foot/coil workflows, and weak speed-to-lead.

## PART 1 — Head-to-Head Route Comparison

**Route A — Direct product-led growth to contractors (AI-native, storm-led).**
This is the JobNimbus/AccuLynx path but entered through the storm-intelligence wedge rather than as a generic CRM. Speed to $10M ARR: moderate-to-fast (24–42 months) if the storm wedge produces viral word-of-mouth. Capital efficiency: high — self-funded, software margins, no channel take. Defensibility: highest — you own the customer, the data flywheel, and the brand. Brand power: highest. Value capture: you capture nearly all of it. Channel-capture risk: none. Fit with valuation-consulting upside: excellent — direct relationships are exactly where you coach owners on multiple expansion. **Verdict: primary engine.**

**Route B — Supplier/distributor & manufacturer channel.**
GAF QuickMeasure, Owens Corning Tech Stack, ABC Supply's myABCsupply, and NEMEON (110+ independent distributors, 640+ locations, "Construct CRM" partnership for independents) show how tech reaches contractors through channels. Historical precedent: tech vendors integrate (GAF QuickMeasure embeds into AccuLynx, ABC Supply, ServiceTitan as "preferred CRM") but rarely capture platform value — the manufacturer/distributor owns the contractor relationship and treats the tech as a feature. Speed to $10M ARR: potentially fast (distributor has thousands of contractors) but slow to close (12–18 month enterprise sales cycles, committee decisions). Capital efficiency: high. Defensibility: LOW if you white-label; the channel can swap you out. Brand power: LOW under white-label, MEDIUM under co-brand. Value capture: shared, and the channel holds pricing leverage. **Verdict: force multiplier, co-brand only, year 2+. A trap if entered first or as pure white-label.**

**Route C — Lean mega-contractor partnerships ($200M revenue, tiny leadership).**
Companies fitting this profile: Aspen Contracting (48 states, 10,000+ projects/yr, storm-restoration-led), the Accord Group ("$1.1B in leadership experience," storm exteriors), Restoration Builders (15 acquisitions, $100M+ projected), and PE platforms like Infinity Home Services ($500M) and Stronghouse Solutions. A $200M storm-restoration operation run by ~4 principals is essentially a marketing-and-logistics machine: it buys leads/appointments at scale, deploys canvassing crews, subcontracts installation, and lives or dies on speed-to-door and insurance-supplement efficiency. What they'd trade for a decisive storm-intelligence + AI-voice + estimating advantage: exclusive regional licensing fees, equity, or tech-for-equity. Speed to $10M ARR: fastest per-deal (a single $200M operator could be a seven-figure ARR contract). Capital efficiency: very high. Defensibility: medium (you keep the IP, they get a license). Brand power: medium (case studies, not consumer brand). Value capture: strong if you retain IP and cap exclusivity. Fit with valuation upside: excellent — these are exactly the owners planning exits. **Verdict: primary co-engine with Route A; 1–2 lighthouse deals as proof labs.**

**Route D — PE platform licensing (56+ platforms + gutter consolidator).**
PE platforms pay for operational tech that lifts EBITDA and de-risks diligence (ServiceTitan's PE program is the template). Precedent: ServiceTitan powers PE-backed HVAC/home-services roll-ups; CRM/aerial-measurement adoption is now a diligence checkpoint in mid-market roofing deals (Focus Investment Banking). Powering the consolidators complements revenue but contradicts the pro-independence positioning — the honest answer is you cannot simultaneously be the champion of independents and the arms dealer to the machine consolidating them, unless you firewall the two (different brand, different terms) and sequence Route D last. Speed to $10M ARR: fast per-deal (portfolio-wide SaaS). Capital efficiency: high. Defensibility: LOW-MEDIUM (PE will try to build/buy your capability or squeeze renewal). Brand power: LOW and brand-negative to your core audience. Value capture: strong short-term, weak long-term (renegotiation risk). **Verdict: late-stage revenue, firewalled; the gutter-PE variant is the exception worth doing early because it's a low-competition proof lab in an adjacent vertical.**

## PART 2 — The Storm Intelligence Wedge

Storm restoration is the fastest-moving, most word-of-mouth-driven network in roofing, which makes it the ideal wedge for trust and case studies. The value proposition is speed-to-qualified-door: contractors report that arriving hours ahead of competitors is the whole game, and pre-qualified storm leads convert at up to 2x generic leads. The 2-minute detection → geolocation → permit-based roof-age/material cross-reference → probability score pipeline is genuinely faster and more qualified than HailTrace (canvassing maps + lead lists), Hail Recon/Interactive Hail Maps (real-time swaths), or the insurer-forensic players (CoreLogic/Cotality, Verisk). None of the incumbents package permit-data roof intelligence into an auto-scored qualified lead.

**But the wedge has a trap:** storm restoration is disproportionately populated by "churn-and-burn" chasers with reputational baggage (State Farm explicitly warns homeowners about post-storm contractor fraud). If you sell to everyone, you (a) train your best feature into the hands of low-retention customers, and (b) taint the platform brand you're trying to build for the independents you claim to champion. **Mitigation: gate storm intelligence behind a "professional restoration" qualification** — require it be bundled with the full CRM/valuation-consulting relationship, price it as a premium tier, and prioritize contractors with local roots and multi-year presence (the Superstorm Restoration / Grex Construction profile, not the tent-in-a-state-park transient). Lead with storm to generate case studies fast; convert those wins into long-term platform relationships.

## PART 3 — The Gutter PE Consolidator Angle

The gutter/exteriors space is a low-competition proof lab for the whole roofing thesis. Named active consolidators: **CID Capital** (Etruscan Gutters & Roofing, its Fund IV's first platform investment, Aug 2025, explicitly soliciting add-ons — the cleanest "new platform" target; VP Chase Williams: "Together, we will leverage the Company's best-in-class reputation... to pursue... strategic acquisitions to build a market-leading residential repair and re-roofing services platform"); **Riverside Company/Evive Brands** (The Brothers That Just Do Gutters, 300+ franchise territories, ~$86M systemwide sales 2023, publicly implementing "a new operating system and customer relationship management platform" per Franchise Times 2025 — the single best-documented tech-gap signal); **Installed Building Products** (Gutter Pro, June 2024, serial exteriors acquirer; Gutter Pro + Thrice ≈ $14M combined revenue); **Great Day Improvements** (Wafra-majority, Littlejohn preferred equity; acquired LeafGuard + Englert from Audax June 2024); and **Leaf Home/Gridiron** (LeafFilter, ~$1.8B+ revenue, merged with Erie Home Sept 2025 in a reported ~$3.9B deal with Ares preferred equity).

**Tech gaps in gutters (specific):** no gutter-native satellite linear-footage measurement standard (roofing has EagleView/Hover/Roofr; gutters lag); generic CRMs don't fit per-linear-foot/material/coil-inventory workflows; weak lead qualification and speed-to-lead. Digital measurement tools cut install time ~30% and material waste ~15% (ForConstructionPros). The US gutter-guard market is ~$0.75B–$1.2B growing ~2.5–4.2%/yr (Freedonia ≈ $746M in 2025; Mordor ≈ $1.16B in 2026); the gutter-services industry is $778M across 4,929 highly fragmented businesses (IBISWorld) — classic roll-up-ready profile.

**Structure options and economics:** Exteriors bolt-ons trade at 4–6x EBITDA, platforms at 6–9x, exits at 8–12x, with gutter/exteriors gross margins ~22–38%. The founder should pursue an **operating-partner arrangement**: (a) portfolio-wide SaaS license (recurring revenue floor), plus (b) equity/carried interest as a Technology Operating Partner (industry norm: 1–3% of profits plus portfolio-company equity grants; senior operating partners at large funds can earn $1–3M annually including carry). No exact "tech-for-equity in a gutter platform" precedent exists — this is whitespace, which is both the opportunity and the reason to structure carefully. **Verdict: do one gutter-PE deal early (CID or Brothers Gutters/Riverside as first approaches) as a contained proof-of-concept, because it's an adjacent vertical with less competition, a motivated buyer, and a clean tech gap — and it validates the platform playbook before you scale it into roofing.**

## PART 4 — The Qualia Playbook Translated

Qualia's actual arc in title/escrow: founded 2015, it built a cloud-native operating system for the transaction (title production, closing, accounting) and achieved penetration by becoming the system of record — 500,000–1,000,000+ professionals, revenue reported around $64.5M (2025 figures conflict; one source cites $113.5M), valuation ~$2.2B, ~$150–207M raised. Once entrenched as the workflow, Qualia layered on an AI system ("Qualia Clear," agentic AI native to the platform) and growth-intelligence tools (referral-network analytics, lead identification) that customers could not build themselves — creating lock-in because the AI only works with full file/state context that lives inside Qualia's platform. Pricing evolved from core seat-based SaaS toward add-on modules (Connect, Shield, Marketplace, Clear) and an AI "Essentials" tier bundled to pull every customer onto the AI ladder.

**Translated to roofing, move by move:**
1. *Become the system of record first* → win contractors with the storm wedge and the CRM built around how roofers actually work, so all job/lead/estimate data lives in your platform.
2. *Layer AI they can't build themselves* → personality-adaptive marketing (Big Five/send-time optimization), AI voice agents, and storm-probability scoring are your "Qualia Clear" — they only work with full platform context, creating lock-in.
3. *Add growth-intelligence* → give owners the referral/market-share/valuation dashboards that make your platform indispensable to an exit.
4. *Bundle an entry AI tier* → get every customer on the AI ladder cheaply, then upsell depth.

**Where the analogy breaks (state it plainly):** Title/escrow is transaction-mandated — every closing legally requires it, so Qualia sits in a non-discretionary chokepoint. Roofing software is discretionary; a contractor can run on spreadsheets. Therefore your lock-in cannot rely on being mandatory — it must come from (a) the storm-lead data flywheel (more users → better probability models → more valuable leads), (b) switching costs of your CRM as system of record, and (c) the AI marketing layer's compounding personalization. You must manufacture the "chokepoint" Qualia got for free; storm intelligence is the closest thing to a mandatory-feeling capability in storm markets.

## PART 5 — White-Label Deal Architecture

White-labeling to a large respected company can be done without losing the company, but only with vendor-protective terms and only while you hold leverage (working tech + outcomes they can see but can't replicate). Precedent principles from SaaS/proptech white-label and source-code-escrow law:

- **Time-limited, field-limited exclusivity** — exclusivity only in a defined field/geography, with hard minimums; "accidental exclusivity" without minimums is a predictable dispute. Cap at 18–36 months with renewal at your option.
- **Revenue floors + escalators** — guaranteed minimum annual payments regardless of their sales effort, with per-seat/per-transaction escalators above the floor.
- **Data ownership retention** — you own all model-training data and derived intelligence; this is the moat. Non-negotiable.
- **Brand-reveal / co-brand-conversion clause** — right to convert from white-label to co-brand (or reveal "powered by") after the exclusivity window.
- **Non-compete / non-replicate covenant** — they may not build or fund a competing capability during the term and for a tail period.
- **Source-code escrow, narrowly scoped** — release only on your insolvency/cessation; the SaaS caveat: escrow gives physical code but often not the right or ability to run it, so it protects them without giving away your live system.
- **No IP assignment** — grant a non-exclusive (or narrowly exclusive) license; you retain all IP, explicitly.

**The leverage point to exploit:** the tech works now, and the partner can see outcomes (storm wins, close-rate lifts, EBITDA gains) but not the methods (60,000 workflows, satellite models, probability scoring). Sell the outcome; never expose the method. Price at rates you set because the alternative — them building it — costs more and takes 18–36 months, during which they lose the storm seasons.

**Exact term-sheet architecture to demand:** 24-month initial term; field-limited exclusivity (e.g., one distributor's contractor base, or one PE platform's portfolio); annual revenue floor sized to your target ARR; escalators above floor; 100% data-ownership retention; brand-reveal right at month 18; non-replicate covenant + 24-month tail; narrow insolvency-only escrow; renewal at your sole option with a repricing right.

## PART 6 — The Definitive Charted Path

**Phase 1 (Months 0–6) — Prove the wedge.** Convert existing live consulting relationships ($5–10M contractors, including the 3-entity owner planning a 2-year exit) into paying, documented storm-intelligence + CRM case studies. Milestone: 3–5 lighthouse contractors live; 2+ quantified storm-season wins (speed-to-door, close-rate, revenue). Revenue target: $250K–$750K (consulting + early licenses). Decision gate: does the storm pipeline demonstrably beat HailTrace on speed-to-qualified-lead in a live storm? If yes, proceed; if no, fix the pipeline before scaling.

**Phase 2 (Months 6–12) — Land the first lean mega-contractor and the gutter-PE proof lab.** Close 1 exclusive regional license or tech-for-equity deal with a $200M-class storm-restoration operator (first approaches: Aspen Contracting, Accord Group, Restoration Builders profile). Simultaneously open the gutter-PE proof lab (first approaches: CID Capital/Etruscan, Riverside/Brothers Gutters) as an operating-partner + portfolio-SaaS deal. Milestone: 1 mega-contractor + 1 gutter platform signed. Revenue target: $1.5M–$3M ARR. Decision gate: does the platform deliver measurable EBITDA lift the PE sponsor will underwrite? If yes, use as reference for further platform deals.

**Phase 3 (Months 12–24) — Scale direct + add the co-brand channel.** Scale direct PLG off case studies; open a co-brand (not white-label) deal with one manufacturer or distributor (first approaches: GAF QuickMeasure ecosystem, Owens Corning Tech Stack, or NEMEON's independent-distributor cooperative — ideologically aligned with pro-independence). Milestone: 500–1,500 contractors on platform; 1 co-brand channel live. Revenue target: $5M–$10M ARR. Decision gate: is net revenue retention >110% and is the storm-data flywheel improving model accuracy? If yes, the moat is compounding.

**Phase 4 (Months 24–48) — Platform network effects + selective, firewalled PE-platform revenue.** Layer the Qualia-style AI marketing and growth-intelligence tiers; monetize valuation-consulting success fees/equity across the installed base. Only now consider broader roofing-PE platform licensing, firewalled under separate terms so it doesn't contaminate the independence brand. Milestone: 3,000+ contractors, multiple channels, valuation-consulting equity portfolio. Revenue target: $15M–$40M ARR. Decision gate: is brand power sufficient that PE-platform deals don't erode independent trust? Firewall if uncertain.

**Sequencing logic:** live contractor case studies (Phase 1) → storm-intelligence proof at scale (Phase 2) → channel/partnership deals that require proof to close (Phase 3) → platform network effects and AI-marketing lock-in that require scale to matter (Phase 4). Each phase manufactures the leverage for the next.

1. *Incumbent fast-follow closes the AI window.* JobNimbus/AccuLynx/ServiceTitan embed comparable AI within 18–36 months. **Pre-commitment:** race to own the storm-data flywheel and lock reference customers on multi-year terms in year 1; compete on integration + data, never on any single feature.

## Caveats
- **Revenue projections are illustrative** scenario models, not forecasts; they assume successful storm-season proof and no catastrophic incumbent fast-follow.
- **The founder's "can't be replicated" belief is the biggest risk to the whole plan.** The individual capabilities (satellite estimating, hail detection, AI voice, personality-based marketing) all exist in the market; well-funded incumbents (ServiceTitan at ~$1B revenue, JobNimbus post-$330M investment) can replicate features. The defensible moat is the integrated pipeline + proprietary data flywheel + speed + brand — not any one feature.
- **Some source figures conflict** (Qualia revenue $64.5M vs $113.5M; gutter-guard market ~$746M [Freedonia, 2025] vs ~$1.16B [Mordor, 2026]; Leaf Home/Erie deal terms are reported, not primary-source-confirmed) and are flagged where used.
- **Storm-intelligence claims of "2-minute detection" are the founder's stated capability**, not independently verified here; incumbents deliver swaths in 2–5 minutes, so the differentiation rests on the full qualified-lead integration, which should be validated in a live storm before it anchors the GTM. *(Dashboard note: Storm Match's Collin County validation — 17× surge match — is the first evidence pass; see storm-match.vercel.app.)*
- **Route D (PE platform licensing) carries an unresolved ethical tension** with the pro-independence positioning; the firewall recommendation mitigates but does not eliminate it, and the founder should decide his own line.
